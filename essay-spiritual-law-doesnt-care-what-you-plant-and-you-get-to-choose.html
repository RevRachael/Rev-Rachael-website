Spiritual Law Doesn’t Care What You Plant, And You Get to Choose

Spiritual Law Doesn’t Care What You Plant, and You Get to Choose

Ernest Holmes has a quote I love that, the first time I read it, made almost no sense to me at all. It’s long, it’s dense, and I had to reflect on it for a while before I began to understand it. So let me do for you what I eventually had to do for myself, and break it down piece by piece.

The quote starts: “There is no principle known to science that is limited by any use that was ever made of it.”

My first reaction was, what does that even mean? So I went looking for something concrete to make it make sense, and gravity turned out to be the perfect example. Gravity is a scientific law, and it works with total consistency regardless of who’s using it or why.

If I toss my keys down the stairs to my David, gravity brings them straight to his hand. That’s a lovely use of gravity. If a child drops a stone off a bridge into oncoming traffic, that’s a devastating use of the exact same law. Gravity itself doesn’t keep score. It doesn’t note that Rachael did something kind with the keys and that boy did something reckless with the stone. It is entirely impersonal. If tomorrow that same child decides to drop flower petals instead of a stone, gravity pulls them down too, with total indifference to what came before. The law doesn’t play favorites, and it doesn’t hold grudges. It just works, faithfully, on whatever it gets handed.

Holmes goes on: “The past does not have a relation with the present and is the creation of the future.” What that means to me in plain terms is that whatever I’ve been thinking, believing, and doing up until now has built the reality I’m currently standing in, and left unchanged, that same pattern will keep building tomorrow’s reality too. It’s not punishment. It’s just momentum. The structure I’ve been living by is exactly the structure that keeps showing up, for the simple reason that I haven’t given the law anything different to work with yet.

The next sentence is where the meat is: “If we break the sequence and initiate a new chain of causation through volition, then the same power that brought bondage will bring freedom.”

In Science of Mind, I lean on the metaphor of seed, soil, and plant, because it’s easier to visualize than pure abstraction. The seed is what I believe. The soil is the Universal Creative Power. The plant is what grows, the reality I’m living in right now. The soil works faithfully, without judgment, growing whatever I put into it, whether that seed was good for me or not. If I plant a seed of poverty and lack, the soil does its work, and the plant that comes up is poverty and lack. If I plant a seed of abundance and gratitude, the soil does that same work, and the plant that comes up is abundance and gratitude. The soil isn’t moved by my good intentions, or my fear, or how badly I want a different outcome. It simply receives the seed I give it and grows it. If I planted scarcity yesterday and I plant scarcity again today, it doesn’t build a case against me. It just keeps growing scarcity, exactly like gravity keeps working the same way no matter who’s using it.

The freedom hiding inside that consistency is the whole point of the quote. I can change what I’m planting. Today. Not eventually, not once conditions improve, not once I feel ready. I can decide, in this moment, to release the belief in poverty I’ve been carrying and plant a seed of abundance instead. Then I water it, because my thoughts and feelings are the water. The soil is going to do what soil does. It’s going to grow whatever seed gets planted and watered consistently. So if I ever want to know exactly what I’ve been planting, I don’t need a vision or a sign. I just need to look honestly at my actual life right now. That is the plant. If I want a different plant, the work isn’t complaining about this one. It’s choosing, right now, what seed goes in next.

Taking responsibility for how I show up, holding my own boundaries while honoring other people’s, and doing the harder work of forgiveness when it’s called for, these aren’t separate virtues I’m trying to collect. They’re what it actually looks like to plant something new on purpose, instead of letting the same old seed keep falling into the soil out of habit.

I’ll give you the same invitation I gave myself. Look honestly at your life, at what is working and what isn’t. Instead of focusing on what you don’t want, turn toward what you do want, and put your love, light, and heart there.

The law doesn’t care what you’ve planted before today. It never has. It’s simply been waiting, this whole time, for you to give it something new.
