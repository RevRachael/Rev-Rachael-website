Where My Thoughts Go: Shadow Work and the One Mind

In 1919, Ernest Holmes wrote a short chapter in Creative Mind and Success about something he called race consciousness. He described it as the result of everything the race has ever thought or believed, and he said we are all immersed in it. When Holmes wrote the word “race,” he meant the human race, the whole human family. That word carries a different meaning today, so I will call it what it actually meant: the human consciousness, the one consciousness we all share.

Holmes was mostly concerned with protecting the individual mind from that collective weight, keeping fear and limitation from seeping in. I understand why. It’s a lot to be immersed in. Over the years, though, I’ve come to realize that there are two sides to his idea. This is where I can make a difference.

If I am immersed in what everyone has ever thought, then everyone is immersed in what I think. Granted, my contribution may be a bit smaller than the whole of human experience.

We say it all the time in New Thought. There is one Life, one Mind, one Power. Usually we mean it as a comfort, that the same Spirit moving through me moves through you. I am never separate from the Source of all things, which is absolutely true. This is a double-edged sword, though. The One Mind isn’t a one-way street that only flows toward me. Whatever I carry flows out from me into the whole.

That changes how I think about my shadow work.

The shadow, as I understand it, is where my wounds live. It’s the tragedy, the trauma, the parts of me I hid because they were too painful, too shameful, or too unacceptable to show. I used to think of all that as private. My wounds, my business, my secret to keep. If I am part of the One Mind, though, nothing I carry is purely private. The shame I’ve never looked at, the grief I’ve never let myself feel, the fear I’ve pushed down so far I forgot it was there, none of it stays neatly inside me. It adds to the field we all share.

Think about a wound that didn’t just touch one life, but touched everyone at once. I think a great example is 9/11. I remember the fear that overwhelmed the Western world in September of 2001. Millions of souls experienced the grief, the anger, the shame, the anxiety that seemed to hang in the air everywhere I went. Whether someone was in New York that morning or watching from a living room across the country, the whole nation felt it. It moved through all of us, because all of us are part of the same whole. I remember sitting in shock as I watched the planes crash, the terror of watching people jump from the buildings, and the mourning of so much lost.

Now multiply that across generations, across all of human history. Every war, every abuse, every loss that was never grieved, every wound that was handed down instead of healed. People have been thinking and feeling into that one consciousness for as long as there have been people. A great deal of unhealed trauma lives there.

When I look at all of that, the obvious question comes up. With so much pain in the collective, what difference can one person’s healing make?

Holmes gives me the answer that makes sense to me. In The Science of Mind, he wrote, “Love rules through Law. Love is the Divine Givingness; Law is the Way… Love points the way and Law makes the way possible.” Fear and hate have weight, and they have momentum, but they don’t have the final word. Love does. I’ve watched it happen in hospital rooms and kitchens and sanctuaries. When I walk into a room grounded in love, that love can hold the space, regardless of how much fear was in the room before I got there. It doesn’t take an equal amount of love to outweigh the fear. It takes one steady presence that refuses to add more fear to the pile.

That’s why I know my shadow work is so important.

When I face a wound I’ve been avoiding, when I forgive something I’ve been holding for years, when I finally bring some hidden part of myself into the light and stop being ashamed of it, I’m not only healing me. I’m taking a small piece of humanity’s unhealed pain out of the dark. I’m putting a little less fear and a little more love into the one consciousness we all draw from. My healing doesn’t stay mine. It becomes part of the whole.

Light helps me understand how this works. If you’ve ever been outside at dusk, you’ve watched the shadows soften and blur as the light fades, until at some point it’s so dark there are no shadows to be seen. The darkness doesn’t remove them. It just hides them. In bright light, every shadow has a sharp, clear edge. The more I live from the truth of who I am, the brighter that light becomes, and the easier it is to turn around and say, there’s one. There’s a wound I haven’t healed. There’s something keeping me from the wholeness of who I am.

When I start seeing more of my shadows, it isn’t a sign that I’m getting worse. It’s a sign the light is getting brighter. That brightness doesn’t stay in me either.

None of this makes my inner work easier. It makes it meaningful. I don’t have to fix the world from the outside, which is a good thing, because I really can’t. I can’t heal anyone else’s wounds for them. What I can do is heal mine, honestly and thoroughly, and trust that every bit of that healing goes somewhere. If each of us did our own part, facing our own shadows instead of passing them forward, we would be healing far more than ourselves. We would be healing the world we share, one courageous look at a time.

If we want to change the world and experience a world of love and joy and kindness and compassion, we must do our inner work. My work helps you do your work, and your work helps me do mine.

What I am contemplating this week is what I’m adding to the whole. When an old wound stirs, or fear and shame rise up, I’m asking myself whether I’m willing to look at it in the light instead of pushing it back into the dark. Every honest moment of healing in my own life is a gift to the entire human family, whether anyone ever knows my name or not.
